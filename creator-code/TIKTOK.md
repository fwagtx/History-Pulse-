## The one thing that makes this account different

**Your handle is the call to action.** `@usecodebad` shows up on every video, every comment you
leave, every search result, every duet and stitch. The pitch is delivered for free, on every
single impression, whether or not you mention it.

That means **your videos don't have to be ads.** This is the trap that kills almost every
creator-code account: every post is a sales pitch, so nobody follows, so there's no audience to
sell to. You're exempt. Make genuinely good Fortnite content and let the name do the selling.

Practical rule: **mention the code out loud in maybe 1 in 4 videos.** The other three just need
to be worth watching.

## Not all views are equal — this is the whole strategy

Two jobs, two kinds of video. You need both, and confusing them is why people post a lot and
earn nothing.

| | **Money videos** | **Reach videos** |
|---|---|---|
| What | Item-shop adjacent, purchase decisions | Memes, nostalgia, gameplay, opinions |
| Job | Convert people **already about to spend** | Grow the audience the money videos sell to |
| Earns | ~$2–5 per 1,000 engaged views | Near $0 directly |
| Post | ~1/day, timed to the shop | ~1–2/day, whenever |

A shop video at 100k views can be worth a few hundred dollars. A meme at 100k views is worth
roughly nothing *today* — but it's what makes next month's shop videos land on 100k instead of
2k. Stop judging every video by revenue; judge each by its job.

**Post the money video right after the shop rotates (00:00 UTC).** That query gets searched once
a day, every day, and early posts own it.

## Money videos — highest revenue per view

These catch people with the wallet already open. That's why they convert.

**1. Shop ranked worst → best** (daily, your bread and butter)
> "Today's shop is actually terrible and I'll prove it."
> "3 items today. Only one is worth your money."

Opinions create comments, comments create reach. Commit to a number out of 10 and let people
argue with you.

**2. "Is [skin] worth 1,500?"**
> "Everyone's buying this. Don't."

Pure decision content — you're the last voice before they hit purchase.

**3. "Don't buy this today"** ← the underrated one
Telling people *not* to spend is the single best trust-builder you have. Everyone else hypes
everything. Being the account that says "skip today, it's weak" is why people use your code when
you do say buy. **Counter-intuitive but it out-earns hype over any real timeframe.**

**4. Item shop predictions**
Big, proven format. Keep it honestly framed as a prediction — never present a leak as confirmed.
Being wrong is fine; being wrong *while claiming certainty* costs you the audience.

**5. Locker value / "how much have you spent"**
> "Your locker is worth more than my car. Let's check."

Enormous engagement driver — people love and hate this number. Pair it with the code naturally:
they're already thinking about Fortnite spending.

**6. "Last chance before it vaults"** ← time-sensitive, use it now
Season ends **31 Oct**. Battle Pass urgency and leaving-items urgency both drive real V-Buck
spending over the next six weeks. This is your highest-leverage window of the quarter.

**7. "Your creator code expired"** (post every ~2 weeks)
> "If you used a creator code 2 weeks ago, it's already gone. Here's the 5-second fix."

Genuinely useful *and* it directly recovers revenue you already earned. Codes expire after 14
days and most people have no idea.

Community building. People who've played *with* you use your code — this converts far above its

High retention because there's a goal. Easier to clip than BR, and you're not dependent on a

Builds genuine loyalty. Loyalty is what makes someone re-enter your code every 14 days.

Humility outperforms highlight reels. "I had the win and did this" gets more comments than a
clean 20-bomb ever will.

## Format rules (these matter more than the idea)

- **First 1–2 seconds decide everything.** Text hook on screen immediately, motion in frame.
- **Assume muted.** Burn captions in — most people hear nothing on the first watch.
- **15–34 seconds.** Long enough to say something, short enough to complete. Completion drives
  reach more than likes.
- **Loop it.** End the clip where it started — replays count.
- **Crop tight.** Full-screen gameplay is unreadable on a phone. Zoom on the action.
- **Bait comments, not rage.** A mild opinion ("this skin is mid") beats a hot take. Engagement
  from arguments is fine; engagement from being obnoxious poisons the audience you're building.

## The daily minimum

Batch-record on a weekend, post through the week.

```
Morning (after 00:00 UTC shop rotation) → 1 money video (shop-adjacent)
Anytime                                 → 1 reach video (gameplay/meme/nostalgia)
Every ~2 weeks                          → 1 "your code expired" reminder
```

One of each per day beats five of one. If you can only do one thing, do the shop video.

## Two compliance notes

- **Disclose.** Epic requires it and so does TikTok. Say "I get a commission, costs you nothing
  extra" in the video, plus `#EpicPartner` in the caption, and flip TikTok's commercial-content
  toggle. Keep it casual — it's one line, not a disclaimer.
- **Never say the code gives a discount.** It doesn't. It costs the buyer nothing extra, which is
  true and sounds just as good. Claiming a discount is the kind of deceptive claim that gets
  codes revoked.
